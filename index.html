01 What I want to study

The gap between a method that works on paper and a system that survives the field.

My projects narrow that gap into three questions. Choose one to see the evidence behind it, from papers and projects alike.

How do you make a perception system honest about its uncertainty, so that a team of robots can act on what it produces without a human double-checking every decision?

  • Hive-NavUnder review A vision-language model lists what it sees. Signed evidence accumulates across frames and robots into a room belief, and a disagreement between robots triggers a revisit.
  • Agri-JEPASenior year project Pre-registered gates and plant-clustered bootstrap intervals. A calendar-clock baseline showed that most apparent field gains were cohort identification, not growth.
  • Localization Under ChangeIn progress A conformal region holds the true position on 89.8 percent of steps for a 90 percent target when calibrated on matching sessions. The calibration does not transfer to changed sessions, and the report says so.
  • Ambiguity or memory failureIn progress Next-frame error splits exactly into the part no model can remove and the part a better memory can, measured against the true posterior.
  • SAMOSAOpen source Every verdict on a derivation step carries a computed bound on the chance that a wrong step slipped through.

How do you combine sensing modalities that operate at different spatial resolutions, different temporal frequencies, and different failure modes, without assuming any one of them is always reliable?

  • FARMSUnder review Texture features from LiDAR height and roughness maps fuse with RGB features. LiDAR alone is the weakest source, yet the fusion matches or beats RGB on all seven dates and reaches 100 percent on the last two.
  • Localization Under ChangeIn progress Odometry, scan matching, loop closures, and localizer poses fail in different ways. A pose graph with a robust cost keeps the parts that agree and drops the rest.
  • Agri-JEPASenior year project Four camera views fuse into one latent and lower held-out prediction error by about 39 percent against a single view.
  • Hive-NavUnder review Evidence from three robots fuses under quality weights into one shared belief.

How do you build all of this on consumer-grade hardware and often missing infrastructure, so that the constraint becomes part of the design rather than a reason to wait for better equipment?

  • UAV crop phenotypingPublished A consumer drone and RGB imagery, with no RTK-GPS and no ground control points. A yawing multi-S flight path halved a 1.09 m dome error, and canopy heights match manual measurement at R = 0.876.
  • Hive-NavUnder review Three Raspberry Pi 5 robots share one GPU over campus Wi-Fi. Measuring 20.8 percent peak packet loss led to on-demand frames instead of new network hardware.
  • FARMSUnder review A 16-plot wheat field tracked across a season without RTK-GNSS or ground control points.

Our six rooms held the same benches and racks.

YOLO-World found no discriminative objects in four of them, and asking a vision-language model for the room name gave confident wrong labels.

So the model lists objects instead of naming rooms.

A hand-built table turns each object into signed evidence for every room class.

Then the network became the bottleneck.

Streaming video from three robots lost up to 20.8 percent of packets. I replaced it with compressed frames sent on demand, with no new hardware.

How it runs

  1. VLM lists objects
  2. Signed evidence
  3. Shared belief map
  4. Revisit on conflict
  5. Assign rooms, then search

Shared memory matters more than team size. Without it, three robots were only 0.9 percent faster despite doing 3.1 times more room-discovery work.

Joint first author. I led the Gazebo simulation, designed the belief fusion and revisit protocol, handled networking for three robots on one GPU, and ran the experiments with Umar Habib.

Method and hardware details
Evidence
The table is hand built and maps object phrases to signed scores per room class. Scores accumulate across frames and across robots, weighted by quality.
Disagreement
When robots disagree about a room, the revisit protocol sends another robot to look again.
Phase 2
The belief map and a room-type hint assign candidate rooms to robots, which run coverage search with an open-vocabulary detector.
Network
I measured loss and latency for two weeks before replacing streaming with compressed frames sent at the pose where each is needed.
Hardware
Each robot is a RoboMaster EP Core with a Raspberry Pi 5, RPLiDAR, laser odometry, and Nav2 with a regulated pure pursuit controller, in its own ROS 2 domain. A GPU workstation runs mission control, the vision-language model, the detector, and belief fusion.

03 Publications

One paper out, two under review.

Published Computers and Electronics in Agriculture Elsevier Vol. 246C, Article 111677

UAV-based crop reconstruction and trait estimation using RGB imagery without external geospatial infrastructure

Muhammad Hussain Habib Chaudhry, Muhammad Ibrahim Rana, Hassan Jaleel

Field phenotyping normally depends on ground control points or RTK-GPS, which is exactly the infrastructure a smallholder does not have. We show that a consumer drone and RGB imagery alone are enough. Dual-altitude capture with RANSAC-corrected orthomosaics reaches 94.95 percent accuracy on plant counting and 91.59 percent on panicle detection, and the derived elevation model correlates with ground truth at R equal to 0.876.

My contribution. I flew weekly surveys of two rice plots for a season and built the photogrammetry workflow. I traced a 1.09 m doming artifact in the elevation model to near-parallel flight lines and wide-angle distortion, then designed a yawing multi-S flight plan that halved it to 0.53 m.

FARMS: field-aligned RGB-LiDAR monitoring system for longitudinal UAV crop phenotyping

Co-author

FARMS tracks 16 wheat plots across a multi-month campaign with an autonomous UAV carrying an RGB camera and a LiDAR, and it needs neither RTK-GNSS nor ground control points. Senior year project, July 2026 to present

Agri-JEPA, latent world models for plant growth

Interactive plot model needs WebGL

Figure. A toy restaging of what a grower could be handed back from one UAV pass. The numbers on the panel are scripted for illustration and are not results. Solid plants are today and translucent domes are a forecast three weeks ahead. Drag to orbit.

Problem. A grower who photographs a plant wants a leaf count with an error bar, a development gap against a sowing-date reference, and flags for stress. Absolute calendar age from one photo is out of scope.

Approach. A JEPA world model forecasts growth in latent space instead of pixels. A small slice of the latent is supervised by a physiological anchor such as leaf count, height, or chlorophyll, and a predictor carries the rest forward in time. Six lab and field sources train under one encoder once each loss is scaled by its own persistence error.

Result. Chlorophyll anchors cut error against persistence from 0.44 to 0.25 in three of three seeds. Plant height raises held-out R squared on wheat plots from 0.570 to 0.665. With no labels, trajectory features recover four wheat varieties across 20 field subplots at an adjusted Rand index of 0.89, where colour statistics, ImageNet features, and an untrained encoder reach 0.37 to 0.43. Four camera views lower held-out error by about 39 percent against one, and forecasts hold R squared above 0.93 out to ten days.

Limits. Not everything worked, and I would rather say so here. The model does not learn a dynamics prior that transfers to a new crop without retraining. Conditioning on management actions did not help in any way I could measure. Several forecasting gains that looked good against persistence mostly disappeared once I compared against a simple clock baseline. Anyone building on this work should know where the model stops being useful.

Localization Under Change, repairing stale maps with pose graphs

Stale map 45% Repaired map 88%

Figure. Scan endpoints of a test session at ground-truth poses, in the window with the most changed cells. Green endpoints are explained by the map and red ones are not. Slide to compare. The map was repaired from a different session.

Problem. A robot localizes against a map. When people move furniture the map goes stale and the localizer can jump to a similar looking place. In 61 of 62 large jumps in the data, the scan really does fit the look-alike better.

Approach. The method builds a pose graph on SE(2) from odometry, ICP scan matching, loop closures, and the localizer's own trusted poses, and solves it with a Gauss-Newton optimizer written from scratch. The map is rebuilt from the corrected poses. No ground truth enters the method, which is scored on the public part of LILocBench.

Result. On two sessions with moved furniture, cold-start localization succeeds on 86.7 percent of attempts with the repaired map against 65.6 percent with the stale one, and on 91.1 percent when the map is repaired after every session. A tracker that starts from the trusted poses is wrong on 1 of 4912 frames, against 31 percent for the filter alone.

Limits. A conformal region holds the true position on 89.8 percent of steps for a 90 percent target when calibrated on matching sessions. Calibration does not transfer to changed sessions. A windowed belief meant to stop look-alike jumps also failed its pass line.

With Umar Habib. Supervisor Dr. Talha Manzoor. The write-up is still in progress.

Deep Learning course project, Fall 2026 In progress

Ambiguity or memory failure?

Turn back, p
Ambiguity A

Hidden frames are where a predictor has to remember.

Figure. A toy restaging of the world. The dashed circles are the two futures, and the real code computes the exact posterior over them.

Problem. A next-frame predictor can fail after an object is hidden for two reasons. The future may be unknowable, which no model can fix. Or the model may have forgotten what it saw, which a better model can fix.

Approach. The best prediction is the posterior mean given the frames so far, so total error splits exactly into ambiguity A and memory failure F. The code computes that posterior from the true generator, which makes A and F exact for every model. Six models share one encoder and decoder and differ only in memory. They are a GRU, transformers with 8, 16, and all earlier frames, a four-block Mamba state space model, and a slot model.

Result. Eight steps ahead with p at 0.5 and no cue, ambiguity is 18.4 for every model. Memory failure is 2.8 for the GRU, 4.8 for the full-context transformer, and 2.7 for the state space model. The windowed transformers fail at 13.3 and 19.2, so their memory failure is as large as the ambiguity itself. These are three-seed means.

With Muhammad Bin Tariq. The write-up is still in progress.

SAMOSA, step auditor for matrix operations, stochastics and algebra

Screenshot. A covariance update with the noise term dropped. SAMOSA marks the wrong row and offers the failing values as a NumPy snippet.

Problem. A derivation is a chain of small steps and the mistakes are small too. A transpose in the wrong order or a dropped noise term is invisible to the eye and fatal three pages later.

Approach. SAMOSA reads each LaTeX step and evaluates both sides on sampled matrices over finite fields. The Schwartz-Zippel lemma turns the trials into a computed bound on the chance that a wrong step slipped through. Gradients and Hessians are checked with dual numbers, and Gaussian expectations with an exact rational quadrature rule. No language model sits in the verdict path.

Output. Each row is marked as holding, wrong with a small counterexample and a suggested fix, a shape error, or not checked with a reason.

Reach. It ships as a web app that runs in a worker in the tab, a CLI, a GitHub Action, and an MCP server so that coding agents can check their own math. It has passed 1,400 npm downloads.

Forma, a browser-native ONNX and TFLite graph editor

Screenshot. A small Inception-style test network with one Conv node selected. The inspector shows its parameter count, size, and INT8 estimate.

Problem. Changing one attribute in an exported model and checking that it still runs usually means writing a script. Viewers show the graph but cannot change it.

Approach. Forma parses the protobuf in a worker, lays the graph out with dagre, and patches the original bytes on export so that weights are never re-encoded. Edits cover attributes, deletion, rewiring, and custom nodes, with undo, a diff view, and tensor compatibility checks.

Validation. The original and the edited model run on identical inputs in ONNX Runtime Web, and Forma compares output shapes, maximum error, cosine similarity, and Top-K agreement. Edit sequences share as URL hashes that carry a SHA-256 fingerprint of the original model and never the model itself.

Scope. Everything runs client side, so models never leave the machine. TFLite is read-only by design.

BAGEL, a browser-native ROS bag visualizer

Screenshot. The topic list and a TF-aware point cloud viewport.

Problem. Looking inside a recorded robot session usually means installing a ROS workspace or a desktop application.

Approach. BAGEL opens ROS 1 .bag and ROS 2 .mcap and .db3 files, PX4 logs, point clouds, and Gaussian splats directly in the browser. It renders TF-aware point clouds and time series with URDF overlays and supports live streaming. Decoding happens client side in WebAssembly and nothing is uploaded.
