01 Current research

Two systems that have to act on what they see.

One drives a fleet of robots through a building nobody handed it a map of. The other predicts how a plant will grow from the way it looks today.

Hive-Nav

Multi-robot open-vocabulary semantic navigation. A fleet finds and classifies rooms given nothing but a natural language description, then sweeps each one for target objects.

ICRA 2027, under review ROS 2 Nav2 Open vocabulary Multi-robot

Hive-Nav coordinates a fleet of DJI RoboMaster EP Core robots across a university wing. Each robot carries a Raspberry Pi 5 and runs its own Nav2 stack. A single GPU workstation orchestrates the fleet and holds the inference models. The mission runs in two phases.

Phase 1 is semantic room classification under dynamic allocation. The workstation dispatches a room task list, and each robot measures candidate path lengths locally with ComputePathToPose. Allocation therefore reflects true reachable distance rather than straight line distance, and no planning query ever crosses the domain bridge. On arrival a robot spins in place and captures frames. The workstation classifies the room with a tiered pipeline that starts at YOLO detection and escalates to CLIP and a vision language model when tier-one evidence is weak. The allocator confirms or denies each room until the floor is resolved.

Phase 2 is coverage search. Once every room is identified, the workstation computes viewpoint waypoints per room from an open-area flood fill and streams them to the robot, which drives NavigateThroughPoses and samples frames for object detection at each pose.

Agri-JEPA

A joint-embedding predictive architecture for plant growth. It forecasts future latent states instead of reconstructing pixels, so the model spends its capacity on how a plant develops rather than on leaf texture it will never need. The target is a grower with a phone rather than a camera rig. One photo of one plant, and back come a leaf count with an error bar, how far behind its sowing-date reference it has fallen, and anything on it that looks wrong.

In progress JEPA World models Self-supervised Phenotyping

The latent splits in two. A small trait subspace is supervised directly by plant age and leaf count. The remainder is a residual that a temporal predictor learns to roll forward. That split is the load-bearing part of the design rather than one ablation among several. Removing the trait gradient collapses the latent to a near-constant vector whatever encoder family sits underneath, so every later extension is organised around supplying that anchor wherever real ground truth exists.

Multiple views buy real dynamics. On the GroMo25 radish benchmark, four camera views cut prediction MSE by 39 percent against a single view, with plant-clustered bootstrap intervals that do not overlap. Multi-step rollouts hold an R squared above 0.93 out to a ten day horizon. A leave-one-plant-out protocol on Komatsuna reproduces the result on a different species, camera rig, and lab.

Leaving the lab is where it gets difficult. Adapting a pretrained backbone far enough to learn plant dynamics erases the features that let it transfer to real field imagery, and a low-rank adaptation sweep makes that trade worse rather than better. Masked-patch self-supervision on unlabelled field imagery moves the frontier instead, recovering field transfer at unchanged dynamics quality. Pooling five heterogeneous real sources under one trait-anchored encoder then reaches genuine dynamics prediction on greenhouse soil boxes and transfers at parity to open-field UAV plots.

I report the negative results next to the positive ones. Several pre-registered checks came back null, including a domain-randomisation bridge that helped static plants and hurt dynamic ones, and they are written up as such rather than quietly dropped.

How the latent is built

Multi-view encoder Four camera views of the same plant are encoded and pooled into a single latent state.
Trait subspace Age and leaf count supervise a small slice of that latent directly. Remove this and the latent collapses.
Temporal predictor Forecasts the residual forward in time, and is rolled out multiple steps to reach a ten day horizon.

03 Publications

Peer reviewed work.

Published Computers and Electronics in Agriculture Elsevier Vol. 246C, Article 111677

UAV-based crop reconstruction and trait estimation using RGB imagery without external geospatial infrastructure

Muhammad Hussain Habib Chaudhry, Muhammad Ibrahim Rana, Hassan Jaleel

Field phenotyping normally depends on ground control points or RTK-GPS, which is exactly the infrastructure a smallholder does not have. We show that a consumer drone and RGB imagery alone are enough. Dual-altitude capture with RANSAC-corrected orthomosaics reaches 94.95 percent accuracy on plant counting and 91.59 percent on panicle detection, and the derived elevation model correlates with ground truth at R equal to 0.876.

04 About

Who is writing this.

I am an undergraduate researcher working on the perception side of autonomy. The through line in my work is systems that have to act on what they see rather than on a map somebody handed them in advance.

Most of my time goes to robotics and computer vision, from camera geometry and structure from motion through to detection, segmentation, and the open-vocabulary models that let a robot be given a goal in words. I care about the part where a method leaves the benchmark and has to survive a real floor, real lighting, and a Raspberry Pi budget.

The other half of my work is field phenotyping. I fly drones over crop plots and turn the imagery into plant counts, panicle counts, and elevation models. That work put a first-author paper in Computers and Electronics in Agriculture, and it is what convinced me that the interesting problems are the ones where the sensor is cheap and the environment refuses to cooperate.

I am looking for research positions where I can keep working on embodied perception. If that is your lab, I would like to hear from you.
